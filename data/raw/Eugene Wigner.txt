Eugene Paul Wigner (Hungarian: Wigner Jenő Pál, pronounced [ˈviɡnɛr ˈjɛnøː ˈpaːl]; November 17, 1902 – January 1, 1995) was a Hungarian-American theoretical physicist who also contributed to mathematical physics. He received the Nobel Prize in Physics in 1963 "for his contributions to the theory of the atomic nucleus and the elementary particles, particularly through the discovery and application of fundamental symmetry principles.A graduate of the Technical Hochschule Berlin (now Technische Universität Berlin(https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Technische_Universit%C3%A4t_Berlin)), Wigner worked as an assistant to Karl Weissenberg(https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karl_Weissenberg) and Richard Becker(https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Becker_(physicist)) at the Kaiser Wilhelm Institute(https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Max_Planck_Institute_for_Physics) in Berlin, and David Hilbert(https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Hilbert) at the University of Göttingen(https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_G%C3%B6ttingen). Wigner and Hermann Weyl(https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hermann_Weyl) were responsible for introducing group theory(https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Group_theory) into physics, particularly the theory of symmetry in physics(https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Symmetry_in_physics). Along the way he performed ground-breaking work in pure mathematics, in which he authored a number of mathematical theorems(https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_theorem). In particular, Wigner's theorem(https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wigner's_theorem) is a cornerstone in the mathematical formulation of quantum mechanics(https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_formulation_of_quantum_mechanics). He is also known for his research into the structure of the atomic nucleus(https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atomic_nucleus). In 1930, Princeton University(https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University) recruited Wigner, along with John von Neumann(https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_von_Neumann), and he moved to the United States, where he obtained citizenship in 1937.
